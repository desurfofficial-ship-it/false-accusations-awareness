Why this exists

False accusations destroy lives, families, and reputations. They also erode trust in genuine victims — every fabricated claim makes the next real victim harder to believe. This archive collects well-documented public cases from many countries where accusations were later proven false or the accused were fully exonerated.

The goal is awareness and caution. Never to discourage reporting of real crimes. Never to deny the existence of real victims. Only to insist that evidence, due process, and the presumption of innocence matter — especially when public outrage is loudest.

Editorial standard

Every case on this site has a clear later outcome: an acquittal, a quashed conviction, a DNA exoneration, a pardon, an admission of fabrication, or an official finding of innocence. Sources are public records, court judgments, and major investigative reporting. Each card links to the public record for full context.

  • Some faculty and activist groups who presumed guilt early

2002 🇺🇸 United States Accuser admitted lying

Brian Banks

Standout high-school football player (Long Beach Poly) with a scholarship offer to USC. Arrested at 16 after a classmate accused him of rape. Faced a possible decades-long sentence; accepted a plea and served over five years in prison plus five years on parole and sex-offender registration.

Falsely accused

    Brian Banks

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Wanetta Gibson. In 2011 she contacted Banks on Facebook. In a meeting recorded by a private investigator she admitted the accusation was fabricated. She later confirmed the lie to prosecutors. Conviction formally dismissed in 2012.

Who / what amplified it

  • School and local pressure after the accusation
  • Plea driven by risk of life-altering sentence as a teenager
  • Civil settlement money that the accuser’s family did not want to return
Outcome. Conviction overturned May 2012. Sex-offender status removed. Banks later played professional football and became an advocate against wrongful convictions.

Central Park Five / Exonerated Five

Five teenagers convicted of the rape and assault of a jogger. Served 6–13 years. Convictions vacated in 2002.

Matias Reyes — serial rapist who confessed in 2002 that he alone committed the attack. DNA confirmed his guilt and excluded the five. Original case rested heavily on confessions later found to have been coerced.

Murder
2007 🇮🇹 Italy Fully exonerated by high court

Amanda Knox & Raffaele Sollecito

Convicted of the murder of Knox’s roommate Meredith Kercher in Perugia, Italy. They spent nearly four years in prison. After a long series of trials and appeals, Italy’s highest court declared them innocent in 2015, citing “stunning flaws” in the investigation and a rush to judgment under intense media pressure.

Falsely accused

    Amanda Knox (American exchange student) and Raffaele Sollecito (her then-boyfriend)

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Rudy Guede was separately convicted of the murder (his DNA was at the scene) and served a reduced sentence. The case against Knox and Sollecito relied on contested forensic claims and media-driven narrative rather than solid evidence linking them to the crime.

Who / what amplified it

  • Global media frenzy and tabloid portrayals
  • Investigative shortcuts under international spotlight
  • Public presumption of guilt before facts were established
Outcome. Italy’s Court of Cassation fully exonerated both in 2015. Knox has since become an advocate for the wrongfully accused.

1931 🇺🇸 United States Historical · Pardoned / Overturned

Scottsboro Boys

Nine African-American teenagers and young men accused of raping two white women on a train in Alabama. Eight initially sentenced to death. Multiple trials and appeals over years.

Falsely accused

    Nine Black youths aged 13–20, including Haywood Patterson, Clarence Norris, and Olen Montgomery

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Victoria Price and Ruby Bates. One later recanted parts of her testimony. Evidence of fabrication and racial bias became clear over time. All were eventually freed or had convictions overturned; posthumous pardons followed.

Who / what amplified it

  • Jim Crow racial prejudice and all-white juries
  • Local political and social pressure to convict
  • Initial media and public acceptance of the accusations
Outcome. Landmark civil-rights cases. Convictions reversed or defendants released. Enduring symbol of the danger of unchecked accusations fueled by prejudice.

Terrorism / Murder
1974 🇬🇧 United Kingdom Convictions quashed · 1991

Birmingham Six

Six Irish men wrongly convicted of the 1974 Birmingham pub bombings that killed 21 people. Sentenced to life imprisonment based on fabricated confessions and discredited forensic evidence. After 16 years in prison, their convictions were quashed by the Court of Appeal in 1991, exposing one of the worst miscarriages of justice in British history.

Falsely accused

  • Hugh Callaghan
  • Patrick Hill
  • Gerard Hunter
  • Richard McIlkenny
  • William Power
  • John Walker

Accuser / Real perpetrator

No actual bomber was ever convicted for the attack. The case rested on coerced statements and on forensic tests (the Griess test) later shown to be unreliable — handling of the men’s hands had produced false positives for explosives.

Who / what amplified it

  • Anti-Irish sentiment and political pressure to convict after a horrific attack
  • Suppressed evidence by police and prosecutors
  • Sensational tabloid coverage presuming guilt
Outcome. Convictions quashed in 1991 after 16 years wrongful imprisonment. The case forced major reforms to UK criminal-appeals system.

Terrorism / Murder
1974 🇬🇧 United Kingdom Convictions quashed · 1989

Guildford Four

Four people — including Gerry Conlon, then 20 — wrongly convicted of the 1974 Guildford pub bombings that killed five and injured 65. Convictions based on coerced confessions obtained under duress. After 15 years in prison, their convictions were quashed in 1989. Gerry Conlon’s story was later told in the film In the Name of the Father.

Falsely accused

  • Gerry Conlon
  • Paul Hill
  • Patrick Armstrong
  • Carole Richardson

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The actual bombers were members of the IRA’s active service unit, later identified but never charged for these specific attacks under a ‘no prosecution’ decision. Police fabricated confessions through intimidation and sleep deprivation.

Who / what amplified it

  • Public outrage and pressure to convict after pub bombings
  • Anti-Irish prejudice in 1970s Britain
  • Police fabrication of confession evidence
Outcome. Convictions quashed in 1989 after 15 years wrongful imprisonment. The Conlon family (the ‘Maguire Seven’) was also wrongly convicted and later exonerated.

Murder
1980 🇦🇺 Australia Conviction overturned · 1988

Lindy Chamberlain — ‘A dingo took my baby’

Azaria Chamberlain, nine weeks old, disappeared from a campsite at Uluru (Ayers Rock) on 17 August 1980. Her mother Lindy insisted a dingo took the baby. Media mockery, public disbelief, and a flawed investigation led to Lindy being convicted of murder in 1982 and sentenced to life. She served over three years before new evidence (the baby’s matinee jacket, found near a dingo lair) triggered her release. Conviction overturned in 1988; fourth coronial inquest in 2012 confirmed a dingo caused Azaria’s death.

Falsely accused

    Lindy Chamberlain-Creighton and her then-husband Michael Chamberlain

Accuser / Real perpetrator

No person — the prosecution was driven by forensic experts whose claims (about fetal blood in the family car) were later discredited. The actual cause was indeed a dingo attack, exactly as Chamberlain had said from the first night.

Who / what amplified it

  • Sensational media coverage that turned ‘a dingo took my baby’ into a national punchline
  • Flawed forensic testimony presented with false certainty
  • Public prejudice against the Chamberlains’ Seventh-day Adventist faith
Outcome. Conviction overturned in 1988. Pardon issued. 2012 coronial inquest formally ruled Azaria died from a dingo attack. Lindy received A$1.3 million compensation.

Murder
1975 🇬🇧 United Kingdom Exonerated by DNA · 1992

Stefan Kiszko

An 11-year-old girl, Lesley Molseed, was stabbed to death in West Yorkshire in 1975. Police arrested Stefan Kiszko, a socially awkward local tax clerk with no history of violence. After two days of interrogation without a solicitor he ‘confessed’. Convicted of murder in 1976, sentenced to life. Served 16 years before DNA evidence excluded him entirely. The real killer, Ronald Castree, was identified by DNA in 2007.

Falsely accused

    Stefan Kiszko — had a medical condition (hypogonadism) that physically made the crime attributed to him impossible; this evidence was withheld by police.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Ronald Castree was convicted by DNA match in 2007, 32 years after the murder. Kiszko died of a heart attack in 1993, just months after his release, never fully recovering from prison.

Who / what amplified it

  • Police suppressed exculpatory medical evidence
  • Coerced confession after sustained interrogation
  • Public pressure to solve a child-murder case quickly
Outcome. Conviction quashed in 1992 after 16 years wrongful imprisonment. Real perpetrator convicted in 2007. Kiszko’s case led to reforms in UK confession-evidence rules.

Rape / Murder
1969 🇨🇦 Canada Exonerated by DNA · 1997

David Milgaard

Gail Miller, a Saskatoon nursing aide, was raped and murdered in 1969. David Milgaard, then 16, was wrongly convicted in 1970 based on contradictory witness statements and police pressure on his teenage friends. He spent 23 years in prison maintaining his innocence. The Supreme Court of Canada quashed his conviction in 1992. DNA evidence in 1997 confirmed the real killer was Larry Fisher.

Falsely accused

    David Milgaard — was 16 when arrested, 17 when convicted of a crime he did not commit. Spent his entire young adulthood in prison.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Larry Fisher, a serial rapist who lived near the crime scene. Fisher was convicted in 1999 on the basis of the DNA evidence that exonerated Milgaard.

Who / what amplified it

  • Police pressure on teenage witnesses to implicate Milgaard
  • Suppressed evidence pointing to other suspects
  • No physical evidence linking Milgaard to the crime
Outcome. Conviction quashed in 1992. DNA exoneration in 1997. Federal government awarded C$10 million compensation in 1999. Milgaard became an advocate for the wrongfully convicted until his death in 2023.

Murder
1959 🇨🇦 Canada Acquitted · 2007

Steven Truscott

Lynne Harper, 12, was murdered in rural Ontario in 1959. Steven Truscott, then 14, was the last person seen with her. After a sensational 15-day trial he became the youngest person in Canada sentenced to hang. The sentence was commuted to life. He was paroled in 1969. Decades of investigation by journalist Isabel LeBourdais and others exposed the flawed case. In 2007 the Ontario Court of Appeal acquitted him, finding the conviction a ‘miscarriage of justice’.

Falsely accused

    Steven Truscott — was 14 years old at the time of the alleged crime and was sentenced to death by hanging at age 14.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The actual perpetrator was never identified. The case rested on flawed forensic timing evidence (time of death) that was later shown to be unreliable, and on a rushed investigation that excluded other viable suspects.

Who / what amplified it

  • Cold-War era pressure to convict quickly
  • Flawed forensic pathology on time of death
  • Sensational press coverage of a child-on-child murder
Outcome. Acquitted by Ontario Court of Appeal in 2007, 48 years after the conviction. Awarded C$6.5 million compensation.

Murder / Arson
1964 🇯🇵 Japan Acquitted · 2024

Iwao Hakamada

A former professional boxer, Hakamada was convicted in 1968 of murdering his employer’s family and burning their house in Shizuoka, Japan. He ‘confessed’ after 20 days of interrogation without a lawyer. Sentenced to death in 1980. He was the world’s longest-serving death row inmate when released in 2014 after DNA evidence raised doubts. The prosecution fought his retrial for a decade. In September 2024 the Shizuoka District Court formally acquitted him — 56 years after his arrest.

Falsely accused

    Iwao Hakamada — spent 48 years on death row, much of it in solitary confinement. He is now in his late 80s and lives with severe mental-health consequences.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The actual perpetrator was never found. The conviction rested on a coerced confession and on ‘evidence’ (blood-stained pajamas) that prosecutors were later found to have fabricated or planted. Five items of exculpatory evidence were withheld from the defense for decades.

Who / what amplified it

  • Japanese ‘hostage justice’ interrogation system that produces coerced confessions
  • Prosecutorial misconduct including withholding and likely fabricating evidence
  • Decades of delay in granting a retrial
Outcome. Released in 2014. Formally acquitted by Shizuoka District Court in September 2024. The acquittal is a landmark in Japanese criminal-justice reform.

Serial murder (confessed)
1994 🇸🇪 Sweden All convictions quashed · 2013

Sture Bergwall (formerly ‘Thomas Quick’)

Sture Bergwall, a patient at a Swedish psychiatric hospital, ‘confessed’ under heavy medication and therapeutic pressure to more than 30 unsolved murders across Scandinavia between 1994 and 2001. He was convicted of eight. Investigative journalists exposed that the confessions were induced, that therapists fed him crime details, and that no physical evidence linked him to any murder. All eight convictions were quashed between 2008 and 2013. None of the murders has been re-solved.

Falsely accused

    Sture Bergwall — himself an offender (he had committed non-lethal violent crimes) but not a serial killer. His ‘confessions’ were the product of recovered-memory therapy and sedation.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

No actual perpetrator was identified in any of the eight cases. Bergwall’s confessions led investigators away from real suspects, and the cases went cold.

Who / what amplified it

  • Recovered-memory therapy discredited by modern psychology
  • Therapists and prosecutors fed Bergwall case details, then treated his repetition as ‘recall”
  • Media celebration of Quick as ‘Scandinavia’s worst serial killer’
Outcome. All eight murder convictions quashed by 2013. Bergwall released from psychiatric care in 2015. The cases prompted review of Swedish interrogation and confession-handling practices.

Theft / Fraud (mass)
1999 🇬🇧 United Kingdom Mass miscarriage · Quashed 2024+

UK Post Office / Horizon Scandal

Between 1999 and 2015 the UK Post Office prosecuted over 700 sub-postmasters and sub-postmistresses for theft, false accounting, and fraud. The accusations were based on the defective Horizon IT system, which produced phantom shortfalls. Lives were destroyed: imprisonments, bankruptcies, divorces, suicides. The Post Office denied for years that the software could be wrong. A 2019 High Court ruling proved it could. Mass convictions began being quashed in 2021. By 2024 fewer than 100 had been overturned; the public inquiry reported in 2024.

Falsely accused

    More than 700 UK Post Office branch managers, including Alan Bates (whose fight inspired the 2024 ITV drama), Lee Castleton (bankrupted defending himself), Seeta Misra (the first to be prosecuted, jailed while pregnant), and many other ordinary people trusted to run local Post Offices.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Fujitsu / Post Office Ltd. The Horizon IT system, supplied by Fujitsu, generated false accounting entries. Post Office executives aggressively prosecuted despite internal warnings. CEO Paula Vennells later returned her CBE.

Who / what amplified it

  • Institutional arrogance and refusal to investigate IT failures
  • Prosecutions pursued by the Post Office itself (a conflict of interest)
  • Years of media indifference until the 2024 ITV drama Mr Bates vs The Post Office
Outcome. Mass quashing of convictions ongoing; 2024 public inquiry findings condemned the Post Office. Compensation schemes launched but slow. Multiple suicides linked to wrongful prosecutions.

Murder
2001 🇬🇧 United Kingdom Acquitted at retrial · 2008

Barry George

BBC television presenter Jill Dando was shot dead on her doorstep in April 1999. The murder shocked Britain. After a year-long investigation, police arrested Barry George, a local man with mental-health problems and a history of obsessions but no history of violence. Convicted in 2001 on the basis of a single particle of firearms residue in his coat pocket. Sentenced to life. After eight years the Court of Appeal quashed the conviction; he was acquitted at retrial in 2008.

Falsely accused

    Barry George — had learning difficulties and epilepsy; was unable to effectively assist in his own defense during the original trial.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The real killer has never been identified. The firearms-residue evidence was later shown to be of no probative value (it could have come from countless sources, including police handling).

Who / what amplified it

  • Public pressure to solve a celebrity murder quickly
  • Misleading forensic evidence presented without proper statistical context
  • Media vilification of the defendant
Outcome. Conviction quashed in 2007. Acquitted at retrial in 2008 after 8 years wrongful imprisonment. The real perpetrator was never found.

Murder
1986 🇫🇷 France Exonerated · 2002

Patrick Dils

Patrick Dils, 16, was convicted in 1989 of the 1986 hammer-murder of two children in Montigny-lès-Metz, eastern France — the youngest person ever sentenced to life in France at the time. He ‘confessed’ after sustained police interrogation. The real killer was serial murderer Francis Heaulme, who was at the scene and whose modus operandi matched. After years of legal battle Dils was exonerated in 2002.

Falsely accused

    Patrick Dils — was 16 at the time of arrest; spent 15 years in prison before exoneration.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Francis Heaulme, one of France’s most prolific serial killers, was identified as the real murderer. Heaulme was separately convicted of multiple murders but was not initially linked to Montigny because police had already ‘solved’ the case with Dils.

Who / what amplified it

  • Police tunnel vision and coerced confession of a minor
  • Failure to investigate Francis Heaulme as a suspect despite his presence at the scene
  • Sensational coverage that branded Dils a monster before trial
Outcome. Exonerated in 2002 after 15 years wrongful imprisonment. Heaulme was convicted of the Montigny murders in 2007. Dils received state compensation and wrote a memoir.

Rape / Murder
1984 🇨🇦 Canada Exonerated by DNA · 1996

Guy Paul Morin

Christine Jessop, 9, was abducted, raped, and murdered in Queensville, Ontario in 1984. Guy Paul Morin, her 25-year-old neighbor, was charged in 1985. He was acquitted at his first trial in 1986; the Court of Appeal ordered a retrial; he was convicted in 1992 and sentenced to life. DNA testing in 1995 excluded him. Conviction overturned in 1996. The real killer, Calvin Hoover, was identified by DNA in 2020.

Falsely accused

    Guy Paul Morin — a quiet, introverted man whose odd behavior (collecting insects, playing the clarinet) made him a target of suspicion.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Calvin Hoover, a neighbor of the Jessop family, was identified by DNA in 2020 (he had died by suicide in 2015). The original case rested on a coerced jailhouse-informant testimony and flawed forensic evidence.

Who / what amplified it

  • Jailhouse-informant testimony (later discredited as a systemic problem)
  • Flawed forensic hair and fiber analysis
  • Confirmation bias driven by Morin’s eccentricities
Outcome. Exonerated in 1996 after 10 years of legal ordeal. C$1.25 million compensation. The case led to a public inquiry that reformed Ontario’s use of jailhouse informants and forensic evidence.

Murder
1970 🇨🇴 New Zealand Royal pardon · 1979

Arthur Allan Thomas

Jeanette and Harvey Crewe were murdered in their farmhouse in Pukekawa, New Zealand, in June 1970. Police focused on Arthur Allan Thomas, a neighbor. He was convicted in 1971 and again at retrial in 1973, on the basis of a single .22 cartridge case allegedly found in his garden. Both convictions rested on evidence later shown to have been planted by police. A Royal Commission in 1980 found police had fabricated evidence. Thomas received a royal pardon in 1979.

Falsely accused

    Arthur Allan Thomas — a New Zealand farmer who spent 9 years in prison for a crime he did not commit. The case divided the nation for a decade.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The real killer was never charged. The Royal Commission found police had planted the cartridge case to frame Thomas; no physical evidence actually linked him to the murders.

Who / what amplified it

  • Police fabrication and planting of evidence
  • Confirmation bias after early suspicion fell on Thomas
  • Two juries accepted the planted evidence
Outcome. Royal pardon in 1979. Compensation paid. The case led to reforms in New Zealand police evidence-handling and is a foundational case in NZ justice history.

Murder
1991 🇫🇷 France Partial pardon · 1996

Omar Raddad — ‘Omar m’a tuer’

Ghislaine Marchal, a wealthy French widow, was found murdered in her villa in Mougins (French Riviera) in 1991. The message ‘Omar m’a tuée’ (‘Omar killed me’) was written in her blood on a door. Omar Raddad, her Moroccan gardener, was convicted in 1994 despite no physical evidence linking him to the crime, multiple inconsistencies in the blood-message analysis, and an alibi. He served 7 years before being granted a partial pardon by President Chirac in 1996. A re-trial was denied despite later DNA evidence suggesting another perpetrator.

Falsely accused

    Omar Raddad — a Moroccan migrant gardener whose conviction became a cause célèbre in France and a focal point for accusations of systemic racism in the French justice system.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The true perpetrator was never identified. DNA recovered from the scene in 2015 (from door handles and an unknown male) did not match Raddad, but French courts denied a retrial.

Who / what amplified it

  • Racial and class prejudice against a Moroccan gardener
  • Overconfidence in a (likely staged) ‘dying message’ written in blood
  • Media sensationalism and refusal to investigate alternative theories
Outcome. Partial presidential pardon in 1996 after 7 years in prison. Raddad has continued to seek full exoneration. The case remains controversial; French courts have repeatedly denied a full retrial despite new DNA evidence.

Child sexual abuse
1995 🇮🇪 Ireland Conviction quashed · 1999

Sister Nora Wall

Nora Wall, a Irish religious sister (Sister of Mercy), was convicted in 1999 of raping a child in her care at a Dublin residential home in the late 1970s. Three were convicted in 1990 on the basis of coerced confessions and the testimony of the two witnesses (who later recanted). Convictions quashed in 1992. The real killer, Jeffrey Gafoor, was identified by DNA in 2003 and convicted. The case exposed systemic problems in South Wales Police interrogation practices.

Falsely accused

  • Steven Miller
  • Tony Paris
  • Yusef Abdullahi

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Jeffrey Gafoor — a security guard whose DNA matched the crime scene in 2003, 15 years after the murder. Gafoor pleaded guilty and was sentenced to life.

Who / what amplified it

  • Coerced confessions (Miller had learning difficulties)
  • Witness testimony bought with prosecutorial deals
  • Sensational coverage of a ‘Satanic’ murder
Outcome. Convictions quashed in 1992. Real perpetrator convicted in 2003. South Wales Police paid compensation. The case led to reforms in UK police interview recording.

1970 🇬🇧 United Kingdom Conviction quashed · 2002

Stephen Downing

Stephen Downing, 17, a gardener at Bakewell cemetery in Derbyshire, was convicted in 1970 of murdering Wendy Sewell, a legal secretary, in the cemetery. Downing had learning difficulties. He ‘confessed’ after hours of interrogation without a solicitor, then immediately retracted. Convicted on the confession. After 27 years in prison, the conviction was quashed in 2002 following a long campaign by the local Matlock Mercury newspaper. The case is one of the longest miscarriages of justice in UK legal history.

Falsely accused

    Stephen Downing — spent 27 years in prison, much of it maintaining innocence and being denied parole as a result. Was 17 at the time of arrest, 45 at release.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The actual perpetrator was never identified. The case against Downing rested entirely on his ‘confession’ given under duress to a person with learning difficulties.

Who / what amplified it

  • Coerced confession from a person with learning difficulties
  • Interrogation without a solicitor
  • Local newspaper (Matlock Mercury) campaign eventually forced the appeal
Outcome. Conviction quashed in 2002. Downing received compensation. The case is the longest UK miscarriage of justice and was made into a TV drama.

1972 🇬🇧 United Kingdom Conviction quashed · 1997

Andrew Evans

Andrew Evans, a 17-year-old cadet, was convicted in 1973 of the 1972 murder of 14-year-old Judith Roberts in Tamworth, Staffordshire. He ‘confessed’ after a dream he reported to police (who treated it as a confession). Convicted on the basis of this ‘dream confession’ and his mental-health history. After 25 years in prison, the conviction was quashed in 1997. Evans had been diagnosed with personality disorder; his confession was the product of his mental illness and police suggestion.

Falsely accused

    Andrew Evans — was 17 at arrest, 42 at release. Spent 25 years in prison based on a ‘confession’ that was a reported dream.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The actual perpetrator was never identified. The ‘confession’ was a reported dream that police treated as fact.

Who / what amplified it

  • ‘Confession’ based on a dream (not an actual statement of memory)
  • Mental-health vulnerability exploited by interrogators
  • No physical evidence linking Evans
Outcome. Conviction quashed in 1997. Evans received compensation. The case is studied in UK false-confession research.

1994 🇦🇺 Australia Conviction quashed · 2014

Henry Keogh

Henry Keogh was convicted in 1995 of the 1994 murder of his fiancée Anna Jane Cheney in their Adelaide home. The prosecution alleged he drowned her in the bath for life-insurance money. The conviction rested on forensic pathologist Dr Colin Manock’s testimony, later discredited. After 20 years in prison, the South Australian Court of Appeal quashed the conviction in 2013 and ordered a retrial; in 2015 the charges were dropped. Keogh became an advocate for forensic-science reform in Australia.

Falsely accused

    Henry Keogh — an Adelaide financial advisor. Spent 20 years in prison. Maintained innocence throughout.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

No actual perpetrator was identified. Manock’s forensic testimony was later discredited; the ‘life insurance motive’ was also shown to be overstated.

Who / what amplified it

  • Flawed forensic pathology by Dr Colin Manock (later discredited across multiple cases)
  • Overstated ‘life insurance’ motive
  • Sensational coverage of an ‘engagement murder’
Outcome. Conviction quashed in 2013. Charges dropped in 2015. Keogh received compensation. The case prompted review of Manock’s career casework.

Murder
1994 🇦🇺 Australia Exonerated · 2005

Andrew Mallard

Andrew Mallard was convicted in 1995 of the 1994 murder of Pamela Lawrence in her Perth jewelry shop. The prosecution relied on a coerced confession (Mallard had mental-health issues) and on flawed forensics. After 12 years in prison, the High Court of Australia quashed the conviction in 2005. DNA evidence in 2006 identified the real killer as Simon Rochford, who was already in prison for another murder. Rochford confessed and then killed himself. Mallard received A$3.25M compensation.

Falsely accused

    Andrew Mallard — had bipolar disorder; his confession was the product of his mental illness and interrogation pressure. Spent 12 years in prison.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Simon Rochford — already serving life for the murder of his girlfriend, was identified by DNA in 2006. He confessed to Lawrence’s murder, then committed suicide.

Who / what amplified it

  • Coerced confession from a person with bipolar disorder
  • Flawed forensic evidence (a ‘weapon’ that didn’t match the wounds)
  • Western Australia police suppressed evidence
Outcome. Conviction quashed 2005. Mallard exonerated 2006 after Rochford identified. Received A$3.25M compensation. The case led to reforms in WA police evidence-handling.

1995 🇦🇺 Australia Acquitted · 2012

Gordon Wood

Gordon Wood was convicted in 2008 of the 1995 murder of his girlfriend Caroline Byrne, a model found at the base of The Gap cliff in Sydney. The prosecution alleged Wood threw her off the cliff because she knew about his boss’s financial fraud. Convicted on the basis of flawed ‘physics’ testimony about how far she could have fallen. After 3 years in prison, the NSW Court of Appeal quashed the conviction in 2012, citing unreliable expert testimony. Wood was acquitted at retrial.

Falsely accused

    Gordon Wood — chauffeur to financier Rene Rivkin. Spent 3 years in prison before acquittal.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

The actual cause of Caroline Byrne’s death remains disputed. Wood’s acquittal does not formally identify another perpetrator.

Who / what amplified it

  • Flawed ‘physics’ testimony about cliff trajectory
  • Sensational coverage of a model’s death
  • Connection to the Rene Rivkin financial scandal
Outcome. Conviction quashed 2012. Acquitted at retrial 2012. Wood received A$9M+ in compensation from NSW. The case prompted review of expert-testimony standards in NSW.

2010 🇦🇺 Australia Conviction quashed · 2017

Gene Gibson

Gene Gibson, an Indigenous Australian man from a remote Western Australia community, was convicted in 2012 of the 2010 murder of Josh Warnecke in Broome. Gibson ‘confessed’ after a 7-hour interrogation conducted in circumstances later found to be unlawful — he was intoxicated, had limited English, and was not properly offered an interpreter. After 5 years in prison, the WA Court of Appeal quashed the conviction in 2017. Police had failed to disclose that another man had confessed.

Falsely accused

    Gene Gibson — an Indigenous man with limited English, interrogated while intoxicated. Spent 5 years in prison.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Another man had confessed to the murder, but police suppressed this. The real perpetrator was never formally identified.

Who / what amplified it

  • Unlawful interrogation (no interpreter, intoxicated suspect)
  • Suppressed confession by another suspect
  • Systemic issues with Indigenous-suspect interrogation in WA
Outcome. Conviction quashed 2017. Charges dropped. Gibson received A$1.5M compensation. The case prompted WA review of Indigenous interrogation procedures.

Rape / Murder
1993 🇨🇦 Canada Exonerated · 2008

William Mullins-Johnson

William Mullins-Johnson was convicted in 1994 of the 1993 rape and murder of his 4-year-old niece Valin in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario. The conviction rested entirely on the testimony of disgraced pediatric pathologist Dr Charles Smith, who claimed Valin had been strangled and sexually assaulted. After 12 years in prison, a review found Smith’s testimony was wrong; Valin had died of natural causes (a febrile seizure). Conviction overturned in 2007, fully exonerated in 2008.

Falsely accused

    William Mullins-Johnson — spent 12 years in prison. Was 24 at arrest. His niece had died of natural causes — there was no crime.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Dr Charles Smith, the disgraced pediatric pathologist whose testimony was later found to have contributed to at least 13 wrongful convictions in Ontario. There was no actual perpetrator — Valin died naturally.

Who / what amplified it

  • Dr Charles Smith’s flawed pathology (he was later found responsible for 13+ wrongful convictions)
  • Confirmation bias toward family members as suspects
  • Pediatric death treated as suspicious without justification
Outcome. Conviction overturned 2007, exonerated 2008. Received C$4.25M compensation. The case led to the Goudge Inquiry into Ontario pediatric pathology (2008).

Tammy Marquardt

Tammy Marquardt was convicted in 1995 of the 1993 murder of her 2-and-a-half-year-old son Kenneth in Oshawa, Ontario. The conviction rested on Dr Charles Smith’s testimony that Kenneth had been strangled. After 14 years in prison, the Ontario Court of Appeal overturned the conviction in 2011, finding Smith’s testimony was unreliable. Falsely accused

    Tammy Marquardt — spent 14 years in prison for the murder of her toddler son, who had died of natural causes.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Dr Charles Smith, the same disgraced pathologist whose testimony drove the Mullins-Johnson conviction. There was no actual perpetrator — Kenneth died naturally.

Who / what amplified it

  • Dr Charles Smith’s flawed pathology
  • ‘Meadow’s Law’-style suspicion of multiple child deaths in a family
  • Pressure on juries to convict a mother accused of killing her child
Outcome. Conviction overturned 2011, exonerated 2011. Received C$7M compensation. Part of the Goudge Inquiry into Ontario pediatric pathology.

2001 🇫🇷 France Exonerated by DNA · 2012

Marc Machin

Marc Machin, 19, was convicted in 2004 of the 2001 murder of Marie-Agnès Bedot on the Île de la Jatte in Neuilly-sur-Seine, near Paris. Convicted on the basis of flawed witness testimony and his own statements under pressure. Sentenced to 18 years. After 8 years in prison, DNA testing in 2012 matched another man, Jean-Claude Narcy, who confessed. Machin was exonerated and released in 2012. The case exposed continuing flaws in French investigation practices.

Falsely accused

    Marc Machin — was 19 at arrest. Spent 8 years in prison for a murder he did not commit.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

Jean-Claude Narcy — identified by DNA in 2012. He confessed and was convicted. Machin’s false confession had been extracted under pressure.

Who / what amplified it

  • Flawed eyewitness testimony
  • Coerced statements treated as confession
  • Slow French adoption of post-conviction DNA testing
Outcome. Exonerated by DNA in 2012 after 8 years. Received €1.2M compensation. The case is one of several recent French DNA exonerations.

2000 🇫🇷 France Acquitted at retrial · 2018

Jacques Viguier

Jacques Viguier, a Toulouse law professor, was convicted in 2002 of the 2000 murder of his wife Suavet, who disappeared and was never found. The prosecution rested on circumstantial evidence and the testimony of a single witness (a former student of Viguier’s, who was later found to have fabricated evidence). Sentenced to 18 years. After 8 years, the Court of Appeal ordered a retrial; he was acquitted in 2018. The body was never found. The case became a French cause célèbre about presumption of innocence.

Falsely accused

    Jacques Viguier — a Toulouse law professor. Spent 8 years in prison for the alleged murder of his wife, whose body was never found.

Accuser / Real perpetrator

No actual perpetrator was identified. The witness whose testimony convicted Viguier was later found to have fabricated evidence; the prosecution’s circumstantial case collapsed.

Who / what amplified it

  • Single witness testimony (later found fabricated)
  • Media pre-judgment of a ‘professor murders wife’ narrative
  • Conviction obtained without a body
Outcome. Acquitted at retrial in 2018 after 8 years in prison. Viguier returned to teaching. The case prompted French debate about circumstantial-evidence standards.

Principles for a cautious society

Five habits that prevent the next wrongful conviction

01

Demand evidence

Accusations are not proof. DNA, corroboration, and due process matter more than narrative. The most damaging false accusations spread because audiences confused a compelling story with a proven fact.

02

Resist the rush

Media, social media, and public opinion can amplify false claims long before facts emerge. The cases in this archive show how public pressure bends investigations toward the convenient suspect, not the actual perpetrator.

03

Protect both

False accusations harm the accused and make it harder for real victims to be believed. Treating every accusation critically is not victim-blaming — it is the only way to keep the system credible for genuine survivors.

04

Hold power accountable

Prosecutors, police, and forensic analysts who suppress exculpatory evidence, fabricate confessions, or present junk science as fact must face consequences. Immunity without accountability breeds injustice.

05

Remember history

Many of the worst injustices — the Scottsboro Boys, the Guildford Four, the Birmingham Six — began with widely believed false claims. Today’s outrage is tomorrow’s miscarriage of justice unless we remember.
